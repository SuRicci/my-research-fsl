## Main rejection risks and disposition

1. Novelty is insufficient for a new-method paper. OSLO transfer, known feature geometry, label-shift prior adjustment and learned weighting are established ideas. Neither directional gain nor source-calibration training constitutes an original robust algorithm. The title, abstract and conclusion remain an empirical failure-study account; C4 is appendix-only. More cosmetic polishing cannot solve this limitation.
2. Scope and statistical independence are limited. OSLO has one target dataset with two image-disjoint same-domain pools; calibration uses previously exposed development domains. Intervals are paired task intervals conditional on those pools. There is no unexposed cross-domain success, broad hyperparameter sensitivity, near-duplicate/pretraining-overlap audit or latency comparison. The draft states these limits and claims none of those properties.
3. Conditional gain can be mistaken for a remedy. DTD-to-EuroSAT is +0.48 pp versus its source-selected control, but exactly matches same-mass predictions; reverse transfer is -1.45 pp. The five-shot fallback also misses a strong control. All 152 accuracy entries for 19 methods across eight cells are written, not a selected favorable row. Extreme prior estimates agree with independent numerical maximization on four examples; the text allows miscalibration or class-conditional shift rather than naming an unproved sole cause.
